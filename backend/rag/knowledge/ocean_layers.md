# Ocean Layers: Thermocline, Halocline, and Pycnocline

## Mixed Layer
The ocean Mixed Layer is the turbulent, near-surface zone where salinity, temperature, and density are nearly uniform due to wind and wave stirring. In the Bay of Bengal, the mixed layer depth varies between 15 meters and 50 meters depending on seasonal monsoon winds.

## Thermocline
The **Thermocline** is the oceanic layer where water temperature decreases rapidly with increasing depth. In the tropical Bay of Bengal, the permanent thermocline generally spans from roughly 50 dbar down to 300 dbar, where temperature drops from ~28°C near the surface to under 12°C.

## Halocline
The **Halocline** is a layer of water where salinity changes rapidly with depth. In the Bay of Bengal, strong river runoff and precipitation create a very sharp positive halocline between 10 dbar and 80 dbar, where salinity rises sharply from low surface values (~30-32 PSU) up to ~34.8 PSU.

## Pycnocline & Barrier Layer
The **Pycnocline** is the zone of maximum vertical density gradient, controlled by both temperature and salinity. When the halocline is shallower than the isothermal layer, a "Barrier Layer" forms, isolating the warm surface water from deep cold water and inhibiting vertical mixing.
