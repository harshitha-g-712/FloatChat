# ARGO Float Technology & Operations

## Float Design
ARGO floats are autonomous, battery-powered profiling instruments. Each float carries a CTD (Conductivity-Temperature-Depth) sensor package that measures temperature, salinity, and pressure as it ascends through the water column. Floats are identified globally by unique 7-digit WMO platform IDs.

## Profiling Cycle
A typical ARGO float follows a 10-day cycle:
1. **Descent** to a parking depth of approximately 1000 dbar.
2. **Drift** at parking depth for ~9 days, carried by deep ocean currents.
3. **Deep descent** to 2000 dbar (the profile start depth).
4. **Ascending profile**: the float rises to the surface while its CTD sensor samples temperature, salinity, and pressure at regular intervals (typically every 2 dbar in the upper ocean, every 25-50 dbar at depth).
5. **Surface transmission**: the float transmits its data via Iridium or ARGOS satellite systems, then descends to begin a new cycle.

## Battery Life & Data Volume
Each float operates for approximately 4-5 years, completing roughly 150 profiles before its lithium battery is exhausted. A single profile typically contains 100-200 depth levels of measurement data.

## Global ARGO Program
The international ARGO program maintains over 3,800 active floats worldwide, providing near-real-time coverage of the global ocean. Data is freely available through Global Data Assembly Centres (GDACs) and services like Argovis. The program began in 2000 and has now collected over 2.5 million profiles.
