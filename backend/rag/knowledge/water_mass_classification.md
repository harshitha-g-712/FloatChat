# Water Mass Classification in the Bay of Bengal

## Overview
Water masses in the Bay of Bengal are identified by their characteristic combinations of temperature (T) and salinity (S) on a T-S diagram. Each water mass has a distinct origin and oceanographic role.

## Major Water Masses

### 1. Bay of Bengal Surface Water (BBSW)
- **Temperature**: 26–30°C
- **Salinity**: 31–34 PSU (very fresh due to river runoff)
- **Depth**: 0–50 dbar
- **Origin**: Local formation from rainfall, river discharge (Ganges, Brahmaputra, Irrawaddy), and evaporation balance
- **Characteristic**: Lightest water mass; forms the barrier layer cap

### 2. Arabian Sea High Salinity Water (ASHSW)
- **Temperature**: 24–27°C
- **Salinity**: 35.5–36.5 PSU (high salinity)
- **Depth**: 50–200 dbar
- **Origin**: Intrudes from the Arabian Sea via the Equatorial Indian Ocean
- **Characteristic**: Creates a salinity maximum subsurface layer

### 3. Indian Central Water (ICW)
- **Temperature**: 10–20°C
- **Salinity**: 34.5–35.5 PSU
- **Depth**: 200–800 dbar
- **Origin**: Formed in the subtropical South Indian Ocean
- **Characteristic**: Main thermocline water mass; forms the T-S linear segment

### 4. Antarctic Intermediate Water (AAIW)
- **Temperature**: 3–8°C
- **Salinity**: 34.0–34.5 PSU
- **Depth**: 800–1500 dbar
- **Origin**: Formed in the Southern Ocean near the Antarctic Polar Front
- **Characteristic**: Marked by a salinity minimum layer

### 5. North Indian Deep Water (NIDW)
- **Temperature**: 1–3°C
- **Salinity**: 34.7–34.8 PSU
- **Depth**: 1500–3500 dbar
- **Origin**: Formed from modified AAIW and North Atlantic Deep Water
- **Characteristic**: Relatively uniform; oxygen-poor due to consumption

## Reading a T-S Diagram
On a Temperature-Salinity (T-S) diagram:
- Each water mass plots as a cluster or along a mixing line
- The Bay of Bengal surface water appears in the warm-fresh corner (high T, low S)
- Deeper water masses appear progressively cooler and denser
- Mixing between water masses plots as straight lines connecting their core values
- UNESCO density anomaly isopycnals (σ = ρ(S,T,0) − 1000) cross the T-S space as curved lines

## Importance for ARGO Data
ARGO float T-S profiles allow automatic classification of water masses encountered at each depth level, enabling researchers to track where different water types originate and how they mix.
